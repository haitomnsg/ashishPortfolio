A small white‑and‑power‑blue robot opens a glass door into a white exhibition of one engineer's work, where three light paths run through the floor and braid into one.

This page decides what the portfolio is. The README decides what it should feel like. When they disagree, the README wins and this page gets fixed.

01 · Thesis

Three paths, one floor.

Programming and robotics both began in Class 9. AI joined at Kathmandu University. The floor of the exhibition shows this literally: three thin light strokes start at one point, spread into three benches, and braid back into one line where the projects that need all three live.

Floor plan · light strokes are 6 mm inlays, blue only where light travels

What every visitor should leave knowing

  • Ashish Gupta builds machines that think, and the software around them.
  • Robotics, software and AI are one practice, grown together since Class 9.
  • The work is real: a blood bank uses RedSoil, restaurants run on RestHat, TomatoBot is published.
  • The little robot is his; the world is his; the projects are the substance.

One sentence on the first screen

Ashish Gupta builds machines that think.

Appears two seconds after Alu, in Geist 500. Below it, a single mono label: scroll to enter. Nothing else.

02 · Colour

A white world with one water ramp.

Ashish's five: navy, ocean, cyan, sky, ice. Blue is light, not paint. It lives in Alu's eyes and joints, in the shaft of light through the door, in focus rings. Nothing else is coloured. The three domains are told apart by shape and material, never by hue.

Light mode

White space, soft contact shadows, glass, a bright key light from above‑left, blue only in emissives and the door's light.

Dark mode

Same building at night, and the night is navy, not black. The key light dims, materials stay, cyan emissives carry the scene. Type flips to ice‑white. No new colours appear.

Retired

The current pink / green / blue skill cards, every gradient larger than a shadow, and tag‑pill walls.

03 · Typography

One family. Two weights. One placard voice.

Geist Sans carries every headline and paragraph at weights 400 and 500 only. Geist Mono, small and uppercase, is the voice of the museum placard: chapter names, dates, stacks, coordinates.

Why Geist

It reads as precise engineering at 96 px and stays humane at 13 px. Its mono is a true sibling, so placards and headlines feel cut from one sheet. Free, variable, self‑hostable.

04 · The robot

Alu.

Named after his own Instagram line, "Kinda like Alu" (alu is potato in Nepali). A compact, slightly potato‑shaped explorer: satin white shell, one wide frosted visor with two soft blue eyes, short arms, two wide rubber wheels. Recognisable at 24 px. Curious, polite, a little clumsy on arrival.

Character sheet v0.1 · proportions head : body : base ≈ 1 : 1.6 : 0.7

Personality in motion

  • Head leads, body follows about 120 ms later.
  • Never blocks content; rolls aside when a case study opens.
  • One wheel slip on arrival. A small chest‑up after a project reveal.
  • Idle: breathing scale ±1.5%, an occasional look‑around. No speech, ever.

Rig contract

Bones: head, neck, arm_L_1..3, arm_R_1..3, wheel_L, wheel_R, antenna. Clips: idle, look, roll, turn, wave, point, present, enter, celebrate, sit. The R3F placeholder uses these names now, so the Blender model drops in later without touching animation code.

Not

  • Binocular eyes on a neck (WALL‑E).
  • A smooth egg with a face screen (EVE).
  • Tank tracks under a cube. Neon. A chat bubble.
05 · The door

A frame that light comes through.

A tall thin rectangle, 4 m by 2.2 m, in the same satin white as Alu's shell, corners chamfered at 45° to echo the monogram's stroke. Inside it, frosted glass that clears from the centre as Alu approaches. The light is behind it, not in it. The same profile returns at every chapter change and, at 1.6 m, as the door of every case study.

Elevation · chamfer 45° · light shaft on floor, haze behind

Threshold beat

Alu appears, looks around, notices the door, rolls up, glances back at the camera once, the glass clears, the camera follows through. 2.4 seconds, driven by scroll, always skippable. It is the only moment that asks the visitor to wait.

06 · The journey

One floor, seven chapters, no rooms.

A continuous white exhibition floor with a long axis. Scroll moves the camera and Alu along it. The chapters are places on the floor, each deep‑linkable, each with a plain HTML twin.

07 · Surfaces

The whole interface fits in four corners.

Chrome is placed once and never moves. A case study is one white sheet. Everything else is the world.

02 · THRESHOLD scroll to enter
Chrome · nothing in the bottom‑right, ever
Case study · one sheet from the right, scene dims 40%, Esc closes

Case‑study order, always

Title → one‑sentence problem → 3–5 images or video → What I built → Decisions → Stack (mono line) → Result → Links. Alu idles beside the door.

Fallback, always

The menu opens a plain, semantic, scrolling version of the entire site built from the same content. Reduced‑motion visitors and ?flat=1 land there by default.

Motion, always

Damped scroll scalar, dolly and pan only, one camera move at a time, nothing over 900 ms except the 2.4 s Threshold. If the visitor outruns Alu, he catches up during a cut, never on screen.

09 · Guardrails

What this site will not do.

  • No skill percentage bars, no badge walls (GitHub already has one).
  • No pastel domain colours, no gradients larger than a shadow.
  • No card grids with drop shadows; artifacts sit on benches.
  • No "Hey 👋", no typewriter, no particles without a light source.
  • No chat bubble on Alu. He does not talk.
  • No software → AI → robotics staircase. Paths, not steps.
  • No invented facts; every claim traces to docs/PROFILE.md.
  • No moment the visitor cannot skip, leave, or read as plain HTML.
